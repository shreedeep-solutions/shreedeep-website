How to Change Your Society Accountant

Changing accountants is easy when you know what to collect. Use this checklist so billing, audit and tax filings continue without a gap.

When should you change?

Common reasons we hear: bills that go out late or with errors, interest on arrears calculated inconsistently, bank reconciliations that never match, audit remarks that repeat every year, and missed TDS or ITR deadlines. The best time to switch is at the start of a quarter or the financial year, but a mid-year change works fine with a proper handover.

Step 1: Managing committee decision

Pass a managing committee resolution appointing the new accountant, with the date from which they take over and the scope of work. Inform the previous accountant in writing and ask for the handover by a fixed date.

Step 2: Collect these from the previous accountant

Books and statements

  • Trial balance, ledgers and day books up to the handover date
  • Member-wise outstanding statement with principal and interest shown separately
  • Last audited accounts and audit report, and the Form O submitted
  • Bank reconciliation statements for every bank account
  • Fixed deposit list with receipts, maturity dates and interest certificates
  • Backup of the accounting software data, with the software name and version

Vouchers and records

  • Payment and receipt vouchers with bills, for the current year and at least the last audited year
  • Receipt books, cheque books and stamp details
  • Vendor and AMC contract list with rates and renewal dates
  • Billing heads and rates, and the general body resolution on interest

Tax and portal access

  • TAN, TRACES login and copies of TDS returns filed
  • PAN, income tax portal login and copies of ITRs filed
  • GST registration and login, if applicable
  • Any pending notices from income tax, TDS or GST departments

Change every password once the handover is complete, and make sure the society, not an individual, holds the registered email and mobile number on each portal.

Step 3: Reconcile the opening position

The new accountant should check that the trial balance agrees with the last audited balance sheet, that bank balances match the bank statements, and that member arrears add up to the total in the books. Differences found now are much easier to resolve than at audit time.

Step 4: Tell the members

Inform members of the new accountant, the contact person for bill queries, and any change in bank details or payment process.

FAQ

Common Questions

Can we change our society accountant in the middle of the year?

Yes. With a proper handover of ledgers, vouchers, bank reconciliations and portal logins, the new accountant can continue billing from the next cycle.

Who decides to change the society accountant?

The managing committee, by a resolution recorded in the minutes.
