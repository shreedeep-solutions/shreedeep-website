Society Income Tax Return (ITR-5)

A co-operative housing society must file its own income tax return every year, even if most of its income is not taxable. We prepare it from the audited accounts and file it by the due date.

Overview

How We Help

Members' contributions to their own society are generally not taxable because of the principle of mutuality. But a society also earns income from outsiders and investments: interest on fixed deposits and savings accounts, rent from mobile towers or hoardings, and parking or hall charges from non-members. That income must be reported.

A co-operative society files ITR-5. Because its accounts are audited under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, the due date is the audit-case date, normally 31 October, unless the CBDT extends it. Our compliance calendar always shows the current date.

Co-operative societies can claim specific deductions, for example on interest earned from investments with co-operative banks and societies. Filing on time is important to keep these deductions available.

FAQ

Common Questions

Does a housing society have to file an income tax return?

Yes. A co-operative housing society is a taxable entity and must file ITR-5 every year.

What is the due date for a society's ITR?

Normally 31 October, because society accounts are audited under the MCS Act. For AY 2026-27 the CBDT extended it to 21 November 2026.

Is interest on fixed deposits taxable for the society?

Interest from deposits with co-operative banks can be eligible for a deduction for a co-operative society. Interest from other banks is generally taxable. We check each investment.
